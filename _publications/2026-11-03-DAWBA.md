## Summary

This [preregistered study](https://osf.io/fs6bg/overview) investigates whether free-text responses from adolescents and parents capture information about the reported impact of adolescent depression beyond that available in structured symptom measures. We analyzed responses from 153 adolescents and 106 parents in the Development and Well-Being Assessment (DAWBA), using large language models to extract information corresponding to a taxonomy of 80 psychological constructs. We then used a residual stacking framework to evaluate the incremental predictive value of this information.

Information extracted from parent free text increased the variance explained in their reported depression impact by up to 7 percentage points on held-out data, with functioning-related constructs disproportionately represented among the most informative constructs. In contrast, incorporating information from adolescent narratives decreased the variance explained by up to 11 percentage points.

The findings are preliminary given the sample sizes, and improvements in the parent sample may partly reflect shared reporting biases. More broadly, the study demonstrates an approach for evaluating the incremental predictive value of free-text clinical and psychological data.
