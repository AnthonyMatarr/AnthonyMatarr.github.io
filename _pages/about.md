I'm a Postbaccalaureate IRTA Research Fellow in the [Machine Learning Core (MLC)](https://cmn.nimh.nih.gov/mlc/) at the National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH), working under Dr. Francisco Pereira. I graduated in May 2025 from the University of Virginia (UVA) with a B.A. in Computer Science[^1]. My research lies at the intersection of machine learning and clinical medicine. I'm applying to PhD programs for Fall 2027.

My Work
------
At NIMH, I investigate applications of large language models (LLMs) in psychological and clinical research. Most recently, I led a preregistered study examining whether information extracted from adolescent and parent narratives through one-shot prompting and embedding-based classification improves prediction of reported depression impact scores beyond structured symptom measures. This work is available as a first-author [preprint](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.11.02.26367115v1).

Since my third year at UVA, I have led the machine learning components of several studies predicting surgical outcomes, collaborating with surgeon-scientists and medical students affiliated with Harvard Medical School, Johns Hopkins University School of Medicine, Heidelberg University, University Hospital Zurich, and Chang Gung Memorial Hospital. I've also deployed two public risk prediction tools, which can be accessed through my [Portfolio](/portfolio/). A full list of my research contributions can be found on my [Publications](/publications/) page.

Research Interests
------
Through these experiences, I have become increasingly interested in advancing generalizable machine learning methods that assess and account for how choices in data collection, representation, model development, and evaluation affect a model's reliability and clinical utility.

I’m particularly drawn to understanding the informational value of clinical data, developing models that accommodate variation across patients and clinical settings, and designing methods to evaluate model capabilities and limitations beyond traditional benchmarks. These interests span applications of machine learning in clinical decision-making, from risk prediction to LLM-based reasoning support.
