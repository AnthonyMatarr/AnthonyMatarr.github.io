I’m a Postbaccalaureate IRTA Research Fellow in the [Machine Learning Core (MLC)](https://cmn.nimh.nih.gov/mlc/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} at the National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH), directed by Dr. Francisco Pereira. I develop machine learning methods for health and medicine, focusing on clinical risk prediction and language models for clinical and psychological text. I’m applying to PhD programs in biomedical AI for Fall 2027.

My Work
------
At the NIMH, I investigate applications of large language models to psychological and clinical research, including the extraction and evaluation of clinically relevant information from adolescent and parent narratives. 

Since my third year at the University of Virginia (UVA), I have led the machine learning components of several surgical outcome prediction studies, collaborating with clinician-scientists and medical students at institutions in the United States and abroad. I've deployed two public risk-prediction tools, the details and links for which can be found in my [Portfolio](/portfolio/). My [Manuscripts](/manuscripts/) page additionally lists the research to which I've contributed. 



Research Interests
------
Through these experiences, I have become increasingly interested in the relationship between clinical data, model performance, and the usefulness of predictions in practice. In particular, I hope to develop machine learning methods that improve the reliability and clinical utility of predictive models by examining the information captured by different data sources, how that information is represented, and how its quality and handling affect downstream predictions.

More broadly, I hope to develop methods that help determine when additional clinical information is valuable, for which patients it may improve decision-making, and how that information can be integrated into trustworthy, interpretable decision-support tools.
